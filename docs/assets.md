# Asset anatomy

Every field on a launch page has a spec and a job. Treat them as assets, not form fields.

## Tagline

- **Hard limit: 60 characters.** PH truncates beyond it — draft to 55 so you're safe everywhere.
- Job: one sentence a stranger understands without context. It is not a slogan.

Formulas that win:

| Formula | Shape | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Outcome for audience | `<result>` for `<who>` | "Launch-day ops for indie founders" |
| Verb-first | `<do the thing>` | "Turn your code review into a demo" |
| Contrast | `X` without `Y` | "Analytics without the dashboard" |
| Familiar reframe | `<known thing>` for `<new context>` | "A flight recorder for coding agents" |
| Number-led | `<quantity>` of `<value>` | "Every launch asset in one folder" |

Avoid: jargon, "revolutionary", "all-in-one", your product name explaining your product name.

Draft ≥5 variants. Test by reading them to someone who doesn't know the product — if they can't say what it does, it fails.

## Thumbnail

- 240×240. Animated GIF allowed — but the **first frame must read at icon size**.
- Job: recognizability in the feed. Logo mark > wordmark > screenshot (never a screenshot at 240px).

## Gallery

- **1270×760 px**, 3–6 images. PNG for sharpness; GIF for motion where the motion is the story.
- **The first image is your real tagline.** Most visitors read only the first card — it must carry: product name + one-line pitch + the most striking visual you have.
- Order as a story: hook → the product working → the payoff/moment of delight → social proof or pricing → closing CTA.
- Annotate each image with a caption that works standalone.
- Never: raw screenshots with no framing, walls of text, stock mockups that don't show your product.

## Demo video

- Optional but disproportionately valuable — PH's audience watches video.
- 30–60 seconds. A **demo**, not a trailer: real product, real flow, no cinematic intro.
- Silent-viewable: captions or text overlays; many watch muted.
- Host on YouTube and embed — don't host it yourself.

## Maker comment

Your first comment, posted within minutes of going live. Anatomy:

1. **Why** — the problem as you lived it, one or two sentences
2. **What** — what the product does, concretely
3. **Who** — who it's for, and a specific first thing to try
4. **Ask** — the exact feedback you want ("does the onboarding feel right?" beats "let me know what you think")
5. **Offer** (optional) — a PH-exclusive deal converts better than a generic page

Tone: a person talking, not a press release. 120–250 words.

## Topics

Pick 3 topics that actually describe the product. Topics decide which feeds and email digests you appear in — reach > flattery (don't pick "Design Tools" for a CI tool to look big).

## Links & landing readiness

- Primary link must be live, load in <3s on a phone, and work without signup walls wherever possible
- PH referral spike is real: test your page under load, pre-warm caches, make the CTA above the fold
- UTM-tag your links so postmortem attribution works: `?utm_source=producthunt`
- Set up a PH-visible offer if you have pricing — "PH" coupon codes track conversion cleanly

## Description (the long field)

- First line repeats/extends the tagline — it shows in shares
- 3 short paragraphs: problem → product → who it's for
- Bullet the 3–5 capabilities that matter; no feature laundry lists
- End with what's coming or the ask — give commenters something to react to
